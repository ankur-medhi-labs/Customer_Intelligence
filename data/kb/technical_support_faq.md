# Technical Support FAQ

## Wi-Fi and connectivity dropouts
If your Wi-Fi connection drops intermittently, first restart your router by unplugging it for
30 seconds. Most connectivity issues are caused by a temporary fault in the regional distribution
node and are resolved automatically within a few hours. If dropouts continue after a restart,
support can trigger a remote hardware reset and push a firmware upgrade to your router.

## Network outages
Localized outages (for example due to tower or node maintenance) have a standard estimated time
to restoration (ETR) of 2 hours. You can check current outage status by contacting support with
your postcode. Outages are not billed against your data allowance.

## Login and account access failures
If you see a "404" or generic login error, this is usually caused by a corrupted local browser
cache. Clear your browser cache and cookies, then try logging in again. If the problem persists
after clearing cache, contact support so we can check for a server-side session issue.

## Device and hardware faults
New hardware (routers, speakers, laptops, headsets) is covered by a 7-day hardware guarantee from
the delivery date. Faults reported within this window are eligible for a Return Merchandise
Authorization (RMA) with a pre-paid return shipping label. After 7 days, hardware faults are
handled under the standard manufacturer warranty process instead.

## Application crashes after an update
If an app crashes immediately after an update, the recommended first step is a full
uninstall/reinstall of the application. This resolves the majority of post-update crash reports.
If the crash continues after reinstalling, please provide the app version number to support.

## Streaming and playback quality
Pixelated or blocky video is most often caused by a full local buffer or hardware acceleration
conflicts. Increasing the client device's buffer size and disabling hardware acceleration in the
playback settings resolves this in most cases.
